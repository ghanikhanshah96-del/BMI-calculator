# **How to Determine Activity Level for TDEE: Simple Guide**

Wondering **how to determine activity level for TDEE**? Look at how much you move throughout a typical week, including your workouts, job, walking, and everyday activities.

Your activity level helps estimate your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), which is the approximate number of calories your body burns each day. Choosing the right level gives you a more useful starting estimate of your maintenance calories.

## **How to Determine Activity Level for TDEE**

Most TDEE calculators use five activity categories, from sedentary to extremely active.

Here's how they generally compare:

| Activity level | Typical lifestyle | Common multiplier |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Sedentary | Desk job, mostly sitting, little exercise | 1.2 |
| Lightly Active | Light exercise 1–3 days per week | 1.375 |
| Moderately Active | Moderate exercise 3–5 days per week | 1.55 |
| Very Active | Hard exercise 6–7 days per week | 1.725 |
| Extremely Active | Intense training combined with a physically demanding routine | 1.9 |

These are common activity multipliers used in TDEE estimates. Different calculators may use slightly different definitions or factors.

Your entire daily routine matters, not just how many times you visit the gym.

## **How to Choose the Right TDEE Activity Level**

Follow these steps to select a realistic activity category.

### **Step 1: Consider Your Daily Movement**

Think about how you spend most of your day.

If you work at a desk and sit for long periods, your activity level may be relatively low, even if you occasionally exercise.

If your job involves walking, lifting, or standing for much of the day, your total activity may be higher.

### **Step 2: Review Your Weekly Workouts**

Consider how often you exercise and how demanding those workouts are.

For example, exercising moderately four days per week may suggest a moderately active lifestyle, especially if you also move regularly throughout the day.

Workout frequency alone doesn't determine your category.

### **Step 3: Choose Your Typical Activity Level**

Base your choice on an average week rather than your busiest or most active day.

If you're between two categories, start with the one that best reflects your usual routine. You can adjust your estimate later.

## **How Do You Know If Your TDEE Activity Level Is Correct?**

Your estimated maintenance calories are a starting point.

If your goal is weight maintenance, you can compare your estimated calorie intake with your average weight trend over several weeks.

If your weight consistently increases or decreases while your routine stays similar, your actual energy needs may differ from the calculator's estimate.

Remember that weight changes can also reflect water fluctuations, changes in eating patterns, and other factors.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Is working out three days a week lightly or moderately active?**

It depends on workout intensity and daily movement. Three light workouts alongside a mostly sedentary routine may fit Lightly Active, while more demanding exercise and regular movement may justify a higher category.

### **What activity level should I choose for a desk job?**

Sedentary is often a reasonable starting point if you sit most of the day and do little exercise. If you walk regularly or train consistently, consider your overall weekly activity before choosing.

### **Does walking count toward TDEE activity level?**

Yes. Walking contributes to daily energy expenditure, even when it isn't part of a scheduled workout.

### **Can my TDEE activity level change?**

Yes. Changes in your work routine, exercise schedule, or daily movement can affect your activity level and estimated calorie needs.
