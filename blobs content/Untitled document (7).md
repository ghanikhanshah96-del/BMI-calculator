# **Army Body Fat Percentage Calculator: How to Check the 2026 Standards**

Looking for an **army body fat percentage calculator** to check whether you meet U.S. Army body composition requirements? The Army changed its assessment method in July 2026\. Instead of estimating body fat percentage with the traditional tape test, it now uses a waist-to-height ratio (WHtR).

You can calculate this ratio at home using your waist and height measurements. Here's how the current Army standard works and how it differs from a regular body fat calculator.

## **How Does an Army Body Fat Percentage Calculator Work in 2026?**

Under **Army Directive 2026-13**, the U.S. Army uses waist-to-height ratio as its official body composition assessment.

The formula is:

**Waist-to-Height Ratio \= Waist Circumference ÷ Height**

Both measurements must use the same unit.

The current Army standard requires a ratio **below 0.55**. A result of 0.55 or higher does not meet the standard.

This assessment replaces the previous height-and-weight screening tables and circumference-based body fat tests.

**Official source:** [U.S. Army — Army Body Composition Program and Standards](https://www.army.mil/tellyourformation/)

## **How to Calculate Your Army Waist-to-Height Ratio**

You only need a measuring tape and your height measurement.

### **Step 1: Measure Your Waist**

Measure your waist circumference at the level of your belly button, following the Army's measurement procedure.

Record the measurement in inches.

### **Step 2: Measure Your Height**

Stand upright without shoes and record your height in inches.

### **Step 3: Calculate Your Ratio**

Divide your waist measurement by your height.

For example:

* Waist: 36 inches  
* Height: 70 inches  
* Calculation: 36 ÷ 70 \= 0.514

The result is approximately **0.514**, which is below the Army's 0.55 threshold.

The official assessment records results to three decimal places without rounding upward. A value recorded as 0.549 meets the numerical standard, while 0.550 does not.

Your home calculation is useful for preparation, but official compliance is determined through the Army's authorized assessment process.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **What is the Army body fat standard in 2026?**

The Army now uses a waist-to-height ratio below 0.55 rather than a body fat percentage limit for its official body composition assessment.

### **Does the Army still use the tape test?**

The previous circumference-based body fat tape tests have been replaced. The current assessment still uses a measuring tape to measure waist circumference, but the calculation is based on waist divided by height.

### **Is the Army waist-to-height standard the same for men and women?**

The current standard of less than 0.55 applies to Soldiers regardless of sex.

### **Can I use a regular body fat calculator for Army compliance?**

No. A regular body fat calculator may help estimate your body composition, but it does not replace the official Army assessment.
