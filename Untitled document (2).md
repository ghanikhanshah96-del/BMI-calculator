# **How Many Months Am I at 17 Weeks Pregnant?**

If you're wondering, **“how many months am I at 17 weeks?”**, the simple answer is that you're approximately **4 months pregnant and in your second trimester**. You're approaching the fifth month of pregnancy, although the exact month label can vary depending on the pregnancy chart you use.

Pregnancy is usually tracked in weeks rather than months because calendar months have different numbers of days. That's why your doctor or midwife will describe your pregnancy as 17 weeks instead of giving you an exact number of months.

Here's how 17 weeks fits into your pregnancy timeline, what to expect at this stage, and how to estimate the time remaining until your due date.

## **How Many Months Am I at 17 Weeks? The Simple Answer**

At 17 weeks pregnant, you have completed 17 weeks of pregnancy. This is commonly described as being about four months pregnant.

Here is a quick breakdown:

| Pregnancy milestone | At 17 weeks |
| ----- | ----- |
| Months pregnant | Approximately 4 months |
| Trimester | Second trimester |
| Days pregnant | 119 days |
| Weeks until 40 weeks | 23 weeks |
| Pregnancy stage | Approaching the fifth month |

These figures assume you are exactly 17 weeks and 0 days pregnant. If you're 17 weeks and several days along, the remaining time will be slightly shorter.

A typical pregnancy due date is estimated at 40 weeks, but the actual day of delivery can vary.

## **Is 17 Weeks Pregnant 4 Months or 5 Months?**

This is a common source of confusion because pregnancy weeks do not divide neatly into calendar months.

A calendar month usually contains 30 or 31 days, while four weeks contain only 28 days. If every pregnancy month were counted as exactly four weeks, a 40-week pregnancy would equal ten four-week periods rather than approximately nine calendar months.

Because of this difference, pregnancy conversion charts do not always use identical month boundaries.

### **Why 17 Weeks Is Commonly Called 4 Months Pregnant**

At 17 weeks, you have been pregnant for 119 days.

Using the average calendar month length of approximately 30.4 days:

119 ÷ 30.4 \= approximately 3.9 months.

That is why 17 weeks is generally described as about four months pregnant.

### **Why Some Charts Show the Fifth Month**

Some charts count the fourth month as weeks 14–17 and begin the fifth month at week 18\. Others use slightly different boundaries.

These differences do not change your actual gestational age.

For medical appointments, ultrasound reports, and due date calculations, your pregnancy weeks and days are more useful than the month label.

## **What Trimester Is 17 Weeks Pregnant?**

At 17 weeks, you're in the **second trimester**.

The NHS organizes pregnancy into three stages:

* **First trimester:** Weeks 1–12  
* **Second trimester:** Weeks 13–27  
* **Third trimester:** Week 28 onward

Some medical references use slightly different boundaries for the start of the second trimester, but week 17 falls firmly within it.

The second trimester is a period of continued fetal growth and development. Some people also find that early pregnancy symptoms become less intense during this stage, although every pregnancy is different.

For more information, see the [NHS week-by-week pregnancy guide](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/).

## **What Happens at 17 Weeks Pregnant?**

At 17 weeks, your baby is continuing to grow, and you may begin noticing physical changes as your uterus expands.

### **Baby Development at 17 Weeks**

According to the NHS, your baby is approximately 12 cm long from head to bottom at 17 weeks, roughly the size of a pomegranate.

At this stage:

* Your baby is moving inside the uterus.  
* The eyes can move, although the eyelids remain closed.  
* The mouth can open and close.  
* Fingernails are developing.  
* Your baby may respond to sounds.

You may not feel these movements yet, particularly if this is your first pregnancy.

### **Common Symptoms at 17 Weeks Pregnant**

Pregnancy symptoms vary, but some people experience:

* Mild discomfort around the sides of the abdomen as the uterus grows  
* Bloating or constipation  
* Heartburn or indigestion  
* Breast tenderness  
* Headaches  
* Leg cramps  
* Changes in skin pigmentation

Your abdomen may also begin looking more noticeably pregnant.

These changes are common, but symptoms should not be assumed harmless simply because they occur during pregnancy. Contact your healthcare professional if you have persistent or concerning symptoms.

## **Can You Feel Your Baby Move at 17 Weeks?**

Some people begin noticing early fetal movements around 17 weeks, but others won't feel them until later.

The first movements may feel like gentle fluttering, bubbling, or rolling rather than distinct kicks.

According to the NHS, many women first notice their baby's movements between 18 and 24 weeks.

If this is your first pregnancy, you may find it difficult to recognize those sensations initially.

If you've been pregnant before, you might identify early movements sooner because you already know what they can feel like.

Not feeling movement at exactly 17 weeks does not automatically indicate a problem. However, contact your maternity care team if you are worried, and seek prompt advice if movements that you have already established become reduced or change significantly.

If you're exactly 17 weeks pregnant, there are approximately **23 weeks remaining until the 40-week mark**.

The calculation is:

40 weeks − 17 weeks \= 23 weeks.

That equals 161 days until the estimated due date.

However, this is not a countdown to a guaranteed delivery day. Your due date is an estimate, and your baby may arrive before or after it.

## **What to Expect in the Coming Weeks**

After week 17, you will continue progressing through the second trimester.

### **Around 18 to 21 Weeks: Mid-Pregnancy Ultrasound**

Many pregnant people are offered a detailed ultrasound around this stage to examine their baby's development.

In the UK, the NHS generally offers a mid-pregnancy screening scan between 18 weeks and 21 weeks and 6 days.

The scan examines your baby's anatomy and checks for certain developmental conditions. Your healthcare team can explain what the examination includes and when it is scheduled.

As your baby grows, movements may become easier to recognize.

If you have not felt your baby move by 24 weeks, contact your midwife or maternity care team for assessment.
