# **How Accurate Are Due Dates for Pregnancy? What to Expect**

**How accurate are due dates for pregnancy?** A due date is a useful estimate of when your baby may arrive, but it is not an exact prediction of the day you will give birth. In fact, according to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), only about 1 in 20 women give birth on their estimated due date.

Your due date helps healthcare professionals track your pregnancy, schedule important tests, and assess your baby's development. However, the actual day of delivery can vary because pregnancy length is not identical for everyone.

The accuracy of your estimated due date also depends on how it was calculated. A date based on your last menstrual period may differ from one established through an early ultrasound.

Understanding these differences can help you plan for your baby's arrival without expecting everything to happen on one particular day.

## **How Accurate Are Due Dates for Pregnancy?**

Pregnancy due dates are generally more reliable for estimating how far along a pregnancy is than for predicting the exact day of birth.

An estimated due date (EDD) represents the point at which a pregnancy reaches 40 weeks, calculated using standard pregnancy dating methods.

However, labor does not automatically begin at 40 weeks. Some babies arrive before their due date, while others arrive afterward.

According to [ACOG's pregnancy development guidance](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/how-your-fetus-grows-during-pregnancy), approximately 5% of women give birth on their estimated due date.

This means that even a carefully calculated due date should be viewed as a reference point rather than a guaranteed delivery date.

There are also two different types of accuracy to consider:

* **Gestational age accuracy:** How accurately a method estimates the number of weeks and days you have been pregnant.  
* **Delivery date prediction:** Whether your baby will actually be born on the estimated date.

An early ultrasound can provide a relatively accurate estimate of gestational age, but it cannot determine exactly when labor will begin.

## **How Is a Pregnancy Due Date Calculated?**

Healthcare professionals commonly estimate due dates using the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), ultrasound measurements, or assisted reproduction dates when applicable.

Each method uses different information to estimate when a pregnancy reaches 40 weeks.

### **Due Date Based on Last Menstrual Period**

The traditional calculation adds 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the first day of your last menstrual period.

For example, if your last period began on January 1, your estimated due date would be around October 8 of the same non-leap year.

This method assumes a menstrual cycle of approximately 28 days, with ovulation occurring around day 14\.

However, not everyone ovulates on the same day. If your cycle is longer, shorter, or irregular, the estimate based on your last period may be less reliable.

You can explore this calculation using a [Pregnancy Due Date Calculator](https://www.fitnesscalculatorpro.com/pregnancy-due-date-calculator). An online estimate is useful for initial planning, but your healthcare professional should confirm the date used for medical care.

### **Due Date Based on Ultrasound**

An ultrasound estimates gestational age by measuring the developing embryo or fetus.

During early pregnancy, healthcare professionals commonly use a measurement called crown-rump length (CRL), which measures the embryo or fetus from the top of the head to the bottom of the torso.

This measurement is especially useful when the date of your last period is uncertain or your menstrual cycles are irregular.

According to [ACOG's guidance on estimating due dates](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date), first-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate ultrasound method for establishing or confirming gestational age.

### **Due Date After IVF**

Pregnancies resulting from in vitro fertilization (IVF) can be dated using the embryo's age and the date of embryo transfer.

Because this information is known, the dating method does not need to rely on an assumed ovulation date.

Healthcare professionals use the relevant treatment records to establish the estimated due date.

Even with accurately known embryo-transfer dates, the actual day of birth remains unpredictable.

## **How Accurate Is an Ultrasound Due Date?**

Ultrasound due date accuracy depends largely on when the scan is performed.

Measurements taken early in pregnancy are generally more useful for establishing gestational age because differences in fetal size become more pronounced as pregnancy progresses.

### **First Trimester Ultrasound Dating Accuracy**

A first-trimester ultrasound, performed up to 13 weeks and 6 days, is considered the most accurate ultrasound method for dating a pregnancy.

ACOG reports that crown-rump length measurements during this period generally estimate gestational age within approximately 5–7 days.

That does not mean your baby is expected to arrive within exactly five to seven days of the due date. The range describes the accuracy of the gestational age estimate, not the timing of spontaneous labor.

### **Second Trimester Ultrasound Accuracy**

Ultrasound dating becomes less precise during the second trimester.

According to ACOG:

* Between 14 weeks and 21 weeks and 6 days, dating accuracy is approximately ±7–10 days.  
* Between 22 weeks and 27 weeks and 6 days, accuracy is approximately ±10–14 days.

At this stage, healthcare professionals assess several measurements, including the baby's head, abdomen, and thigh bone.

If an earlier reliable ultrasound has already established the due date, a later scan usually does not replace it simply because the baby's measurements differ.

### **Third Trimester Ultrasound Accuracy**

Ultrasound is least reliable for establishing gestational age during the third trimester.

ACOG reports a dating accuracy of approximately ±21–30 days after 28 weeks.

By this point, normal differences in fetal growth can make it difficult to distinguish a baby's individual size from the pregnancy's actual gestational age.

For that reason, healthcare professionals generally rely on the earliest reliable dating information rather than repeatedly changing the due date based on later growth scans.

## **Why Pregnancy Due Dates Can Be Wrong**

An estimated due date may differ from the most accurate clinical estimate for several reasons.

### **Irregular Menstrual Cycles**

The standard last-period calculation assumes ovulation around day 14 of a 28-day cycle.

Someone with a 35-day cycle may ovulate later than this assumption suggests, while someone with a shorter cycle may ovulate earlier.

As a result, the last-period estimate may not match the gestational age established by ultrasound.

### **Uncertain Last Period Date**

Some people cannot remember the exact first day of their last menstrual period.

Light bleeding, irregular cycles, or uncertainty about whether bleeding was a normal period can also make menstrual dating less reliable.

An early ultrasound may help establish a more dependable estimate.

### **Differences in Ovulation Timing**

Even people with fairly regular cycles do not necessarily ovulate on exactly the same day every month.

Because fertilization generally occurs around ovulation, variation in its timing can affect calculations that assume a fixed conception date.

### **Late Pregnancy Ultrasound Measurements**

A baby measuring larger or smaller than average during a later scan does not necessarily mean the original due date was incorrect.

Differences in fetal growth can influence ultrasound measurements, especially in the third trimester.

Healthcare professionals consider the pregnancy's dating history and clinical findings before deciding whether a due date should change.

## **How Often Are Babies Born on Their Due Date?**

Only a small proportion of babies arrive on their exact estimated due date.

ACOG states that approximately 1 in 20 women give birth on the predicted date.

This is why an estimated due date should not be treated as a fixed appointment.

Some pregnancies reach full term before 40 weeks, while others continue beyond that point.

For clinical purposes, pregnancy timing is commonly described using these categories:

| Pregnancy duration | Classification |
| ----- | ----- |
| Before 37 weeks | Preterm |
| 37 weeks to 38 weeks, 6 days | Early term |
| 39 weeks to 40 weeks, 6 days | Full term |
| 41 weeks to 41 weeks, 6 days | Late term |
| 42 weeks and beyond | Postterm |

These categories help healthcare professionals describe pregnancy duration and guide care.

They do not mean that every pregnancy will follow the same timeline or that delivery at any point in these ranges carries identical risks.

## **Can Your Pregnancy Due Date Change?**

Yes. A healthcare professional may revise an estimated due date when more reliable dating information becomes available.

For example, an early ultrasound might show that the pregnancy is several days earlier or later than expected from the last menstrual period.

Whether the date should change depends on the timing of the ultrasound, the size of the discrepancy, and how reliable the original menstrual dating information is.

However, due dates are not normally changed after every ultrasound.

ACOG recommends establishing and documenting the best estimated due date early in pregnancy. Subsequent changes should be reserved for relatively uncommon circumstances and discussed with the patient.

If your due date changes, ask your healthcare professional which measurement was used and why the revised estimate is considered more reliable.

## **Which Pregnancy Due Date Method Is Most Accurate?**

The most appropriate dating method depends on the information available and how early it was obtained.

| Dating method | Main consideration |
| ----- | ----- |
| Last menstrual period | Useful when the date is known and cycles are regular |
| First-trimester ultrasound | Most accurate ultrasound method for establishing gestational age |
| Second-trimester ultrasound | Useful when earlier reliable dating is unavailable, but less precise |
| Third-trimester ultrasound | Least reliable for establishing gestational age |
| IVF embryo-transfer dating | Uses known embryo age and transfer date |

If the last menstrual period and early ultrasound suggest different dates, healthcare professionals use established clinical guidelines to determine which estimate should be used.

A calculator can provide a helpful starting point, but it cannot replace ultrasound findings or individualized clinical assessment.

## **What If Your Due Date Passes and You Are Still Pregnant?**

Reaching your estimated due date without going into labor does not automatically mean something is wrong.

However, the timing of prenatal monitoring and any discussion about induction becomes more important as pregnancy continues.

Your healthcare professional may recommend additional assessments or discuss delivery planning based on your gestational age, medical history, and individual circumstances.

Do not change your due date yourself simply because labor has not started.

If you are past your estimated due date, contact your maternity care team for advice about your next appointment and any recommended monitoring. Seek prompt medical assessment for reduced fetal movements, vaginal bleeding, or other concerning symptoms, regardless of your due date.

Due dates can be useful estimates of gestational age, especially when established through an early ultrasound. However, they are not highly accurate at predicting the exact day of birth. Only about 5% of women give birth on their estimated due date, according to ACOG.

### **Is the ultrasound due date more accurate than the last period?**

A first-trimester ultrasound is generally the most accurate ultrasound method for estimating gestational age. ### **How many days can a pregnancy due date be off?**

The uncertainty depends on the dating method and when it is performed. First-trimester ultrasound dating generally has an accuracy of approximately ±5–7 days, while later ultrasound dating is less precise. These ranges describe uncertainty in gestational age, not how many days before or after the due date labor will occur.

### **Can a due date change by two weeks?**

It can, particularly when the original estimate was based on uncertain menstrual dates and later clinical information indicates a meaningful difference. Whether a change is appropriate depends on the timing and reliability of the ultrasound and other dating information.

A due date calculator can correctly apply a standard formula to the information entered. Its usefulness depends on the accuracy of that information and whether the assumptions fit your menstrual cycle. It cannot predict the exact day labor will begin.

Yes. Babies can be born before or after their estimated due date. The date is used to guide pregnancy care, but the timing of delivery varies from one pregnancy to another.
